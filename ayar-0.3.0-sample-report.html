What the flags mean

CONTAMINATION · 2 flag(s)

Why it matters: the unit mixes several neurons, so its tuning, rate changes and correlations are partly another neuron's

Fix: re-curate the unit (split it in Phy, or exclude it); if a source unit is named, check whether the two should be re-sorted together

DRIFT_CONFOUND · 3 flag(s)

Why it matters: the unit's rate changes over the session because spikes are lost below the detection threshold, not because the neuron changed; conditions are ordered in time (r=-0.87 for condition '0'), so this loss shows up as a condition effect

Fix: apply motion correction before sorting (SpikeInterface correct_motion, Kilosort drift correction), restrict each unit to the epoch where its amplitude is stable, or exclude it; interleave conditions in future designs

DUPLICATE_UNIT · 2 flag(s)

Why it matters: inflates the number of neurons, doubles this neuron's weight in population averages and decoders, and creates a fake zero-lag correlation

Fix: merge each pair (Phy merge, or SpikeInterface remove_redundant_units) and re-run the analysis

OVERLAP_HOLE · 2 flag(s)

Why it matters: zero-lag synchrony between these units is underestimated; either the sorter missed overlapping spikes, or the two units are one neuron split in two

Fix: inspect each pair's cross-correlogram and templates; merge pairs whose templates match, otherwise exclude the pair from synchrony and correlation analyses

SPLIT_UNIT · 3 flag(s)

Why it matters: one neuron is counted twice; a handoff also looks like one neuron switching off while another switches on (fake remapping, plasticity or cell loss)

Fix: merge each pair (Phy merge, SpikeInterface apply_curation with a merge) after checking that the templates match, then re-run the analysis
